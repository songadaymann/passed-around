A LITTLE APP. A VERY CLEAR BOUNDARY.

Your chat stays private.
Your music can travel.

The open-source Mac app finds music links in a conversation you choose. You review them before publishing. Only the selected music URLs leave your Mac.

  1. Choose your conversation.Messages is opened read-only, on your Mac.
  2. Review what it found.Keep it local, export it, or select songs to publish.
  3. Pass on the good stuff.No chat names, contacts, or message text are sent.

Installing the developer preview

This first build is ad-hoc signed and has not been notarized by Apple. Unzip it and move Passed Around to Applications. If macOS blocks opening it, inspect the source or build it yourself; after attempting to open it, you can also use System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.

When connecting Messages, select your Messages folder. If macOS still denies access, grant the app Full Disk Access and quit and reopen it. The app explains this before scanning. Download checksums.

PLAIN WORDS, OPEN CODE

The music leaves.
The conversation stays.

The Mac app has access to your Messages database after you grant permission. It restricts scanning to the conversation you select. macOS does not provide a separate permission for just that chat’s music links.

What gets sent

Only URLs you select, using a random collection key. No group names, participant identities, message bodies, original message dates, or device identifiers.

What becomes public

The songs, their listening links, and how many distinct contributing groups have shared each one. Group keys and identities are never listed. Publish only music your group is comfortable contributing. You can remove your collection’s contributions from the app.

How listening links are found

Original links are marked “Shared.” Additional Apple Music and Deezer links are marked “Metadata match” only when artist, full title, and duration agree. This is a conservative match, not a guaranteed recording identifier. Missing platforms have explicitly labeled search links. YouTube uploads are not assumed to identify the artist, and Bandcamp is not scraped.

Music providers receive public music URLs or artist/title searches during matching. The site has no analytics or advertising scripts. Cloudflare still handles ordinary network information such as IP addresses when serving requests. Album artwork loads from the providers’ image servers.

Your controls

Scanning is local. Publishing is a separate action. Automatic scanning is optional and never automatically publishes new finds. Collection credentials stay in macOS Keychain. Deleting a collection removes its contributions; shared tracks remain only if another collection contributed them.
